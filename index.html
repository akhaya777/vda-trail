The journey

Four hops. Two of them are taxable.

The coloured bar on the left of each step tells you what you are holding at that moment. Blue is your crypto, green is USDT, amber is rupees. Steps 1 and 2 share the same blue because nothing was sold — you only moved your own coins. So a taxable transfer is exactly where the colour changes, and it changes twice.

  1. 01
    Crypto lands in your walletCrypto

    You paste the on-chain tx hash. The app pulls the arrival time and what the asset was worth in rupees at that moment. That figure is your cost of acquisition, and everything else is measured against it.

  2. 02
    You send it to BinanceCrypto

    Another tx hash. Nothing was sold here, you just moved your own coins between two places you control, so there's no tax. The hop is here to show it's the same batch that arrives at the exchange.

  3. 03
    You convert it to USDTUSDT

    Taxable transfer 1 of 2 · crypto → USDT

    Swapping one coin for another counts as a sale in India, even though no rupees moved. Your cost is the hop 1 value. Your sale consideration is what the USDT was worth when you got it.

    Binance keeps this off the public chain, so it comes from your trade history export or you type it in.

  4. 04
    You sell the USDT for rupeesINR

    Taxable transfer 2 of 2 · USDT → INR

    A second sale, judged entirely on its own. Cost is what the USDT was worth when you acquired it. Sale consideration is what the buyer actually paid into your bank.

    From your P2P order history. This is also the leg carrying the 1% TDS that you have to deposit yourself.

The tax logic

Three rules the engine refuses to break.

Flat 31.2%

30% + 4% cess, no exceptions

Every gain is taxed at 30% plus a 4% health & education cess — an effective 31.2%. There is no short-term versus long-term split and no holding-period benefit. Holding for three years buys you nothing.

No set-off

A loss on one leg cannot touch a gain on another

If Event #1 is a gain and Event #2 is a loss, you still pay 30% on the full gain and the loss simply vanishes. It cannot be carried forward either. Each taxable leg stands completely alone.

Cost only

Fees are not deductible

Gas, Binance trading fees and P2P spread come out of your pocket but not out of your taxable gain. SEREVIA records them so you know your real economics — and never subtracts them in the tax maths.

Why timing is the whole point

The gain on each leg is the price difference between two moments in time. That is why every hop stores its own precise timestamp and its own INR price. A few hours can move the price, and therefore the tax. The app will never reuse today's price for a trade that happened three months ago.

The 1% TDS is on you

Section 194S puts a 1% TDS on VDA transfers. An Indian exchange usually deducts it for you. Binance P2P does not — for a P2P trade on an international platform, you are typically the one who has to deduct and deposit it.

SEREVIA computes it on the sale consideration of the P2P leg and labels it payable by you, never already deducted. It is an advance tax: it is credited against your 30% liability, not charged on top of it.

A boundary worth knowing

If you hold crypto on a foreign platform and the total value crosses ₹20 lakh, it may also need disclosure in Schedule FA (Foreign Assets) — a separate obligation from Schedule VDA.

The Tax Summary raises a flag when your year crosses that line. It does not compute Schedule FA for you; that is a conversation with your CA.

What the tool must never do here

Total tax is ₹6,240 — not ₹6,240 minus something for the ₹1,000 P2P loss. That loss is legally invisible; it cannot reduce the gain on Event #1. A naive calculator that nets the two legs into a ₹19,000 gain would report ₹5,928 and under-state your tax by ₹312, which is exactly the kind of thing that earns a notice. Avoiding that is the reason this tool exists.

Privacy is the whole point

This holds your complete crypto-to-cash trail, so it stores everything locally — in this build, in your own browser's storage on this machine. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account and there is no server.

What this app will never ask for

No private key. No seed phrase. No Binance password. No API secret with withdrawal rights. It needs only public addresses, public tx hashes and read-only exported history. If a field could hold a secret that moves funds, it does not belong in this tool — and you should distrust any crypto tax tool that asks.

Design note

Why the trails are not stored on-chain.

SEREVIA reads the public blockchain but keeps everything in a private local store. Putting your tax trails into a smart contract instead would be a mistake on four counts.

Privacy

A public chain publishes your finances permanently and to everyone. Writing your income, your bank-facing P2P sales and your wallet linkages on-chain would tie your real identity to every rupee you have ever moved, with no way to take it back.

Immutability cuts the wrong way

Tax records get corrected — a mis-typed order ID, a hop classified as a sale when it was a transfer, a price fetched for the wrong date. A local record is edited. An on-chain record can only be appended to, leaving your mistake permanently visible next to the fix.

Cost for nothing

Every write costs gas. You would be paying real money, per edit, to store data that only you will ever read — and paying it again each time you correct a figure during filing season.

Wrong tool for the job

A blockchain's actual value is trustless shared state between strangers who cannot agree on a referee. A personal tax ledger has exactly one user, who already trusts themselves. There is no disagreement for consensus to resolve, so all you keep is the overhead.

What this tool is — and isn't

SEREVIA produces an estimate to help you and your CA. It is not tax advice and it is not a filing service. Tax law changes, and edge cases — gifts, airdrops landing in the wallet, staking rewards, the ₹20 lakh Schedule FA threshold — sit outside this flow and need a professional. Have a CA review the numbers before you file. The tool exists to make you well-informed, not to replace expert judgement.

Settings

Lookups, keys and your saved data

Everything below is optional. Out of the box the app reads public blockchain nodes and public price feeds with no key and no account, so pasting a tx hash just works. These boxes are for people who want to point it somewhere else.

What it reads, with no key

Public nodes on Ethereum, Polygon, BNB Chain, Arbitrum, Base and Optimism give the timestamp, the token and the amount. Binance's public price feed gives what that token was worth at the exact minute of your hop, and the ECB reference rate converts it to rupees. Several nodes are tried per chain, so one being down doesn't stop you.

Never paste a key that can move money

Nothing here needs one. A Binance API secret can move your funds and must never go in this app, in this box or any other. If a tax tool ever asks you for one, it's asking for far more than it needs to do the job.

Sign-in

The password is never stored. What is kept is a salted hash of it, produced by 50,000 rounds of SHA-256, so the password itself cannot be read back out of this browser.

Your own node

Tried first, with the public nodes as fallback. Useful if you run your own node or have an Infura/Alchemy URL. Solana and Bitcoin aren't covered by automatic lookup — enter those hops by hand.

Pricing fallback

CoinGecko gives a daily average in INR directly. It's the backstop for assets Binance doesn't list — the free tier is rate-limited, so a key helps there.

Your data

Everything lives in this browser on this machine. That is good for privacy and bad for accident recovery — clearing site data wipes your trails. Take a backup before filing season.
